# SPI-M yardstick calibration

During COVID-19, SPI-M-O, the modelling subgroup of the UK's Scientific Advisory Group for Emergencies (SAGE), wrote its consensus statements with verbal probability terms such as "likely", "highly likely" and "realistic possibility". The terms come from the probability yardstick of the Professional Head of Intelligence Assessment (PHIA), which gives each one a numeric range: "likely", for example, means 55–75%. This study asks whether the statements came true as often as their terms said. When SPI-M-O wrote "likely", was it right about 55–75% of the time?

Each statement is stripped of its probability term and shown to independent reviewers as a bare proposition. They judge, with hindsight, whether it turned out correct, without seeing the term. Grouping their verdicts by the withheld term gives the calibration.

The survey is ready to deploy. It has 309 items: 291 statements from February 2020 to February 2022 and 18 hidden quality-control items.

## Pipeline

The scripts in `scripts/` are run from the repository root and read and write paths under `data/`. In rough order:

1. `list_statements.py` lists the SPI-M-O consensus statements and their attachments through the gov.uk search and content APIs, into `data/consensus_with_attachments.json`. It uses the search API because the monthly SAGE collections miss about 12% of the statements, all of August 2021 among them. `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` sets out the scope.
2. `download.py` downloads the PDFs of the statements (rate-limited, with an identifying User-Agent) and extracts their text with `pdftotext -layout` (poppler 26.01; other versions can place text differently). It writes `data/pdfs/`, `data/txt/` and `data/manifest.json`, the provenance record with SHA-256 checksums.
3. `scan.py`, `claims.py`, `classify.py`, `tense.py`, `make_frame.py` and `dedupe_frame.py` extract candidate yardstick statements, classify them by resolvability (whether a statement can be judged true or false at all), and filter and deduplicate them into the coding frame, `data/coding_frame_dedup.csv`.
4. `build_lineage.py`, `select_new_claims.py` and `build_strip_payload.py` link each claim in the frame to its candidates, select the candidates outside the frame for a second run, and build the claim payloads for both stripping runs.
5. `wf_strip.js` and `wf_strip_new.js` are multi-agent workflows, run with the payloads as their `args`. They strip the probability wording to leave a bare, self-contained proposition, split compound statements, and check each proposition against its source with an adversarial verifier. Their output, `data/strip_results.json` and `data/strip_results_new.json`, cannot be regenerated exactly and is archived with the answer key (below). `build_propositions.py`, `finalise_propositions.py` (the edits made on review) and `build_new_props.py` assemble the propositions.
6. `scenario_detailing.py` applies the decisions on named model scenarios (for example omicron A–F) recorded in `data/scenario_decisions.json`: state the claim that distinguishes the scenario, drop figure captions, or label references too vague to resolve.
7. `build_master.py` merges everything, with the quality-control items from `data/controls_key.json`, into `data/propositions_master.csv`, the full item set with the band and resolvability class of each item.
8. `build_survey_master.py` produces the survey file and the item map (below).
9. `build_ledger.py` writes the audit trail (below).

`textnorm.py` holds the sentence normalisation that the matching scripts share. `exclusions.py` lists the items dropped by a scope rule or on review. The `gen_jisc`, `gen_native`, `build_sheet*`, `build_final`, `build_survey_final`, `rclaims` and `contexts` scripts and the `wf_annotate*.js` workflows are exploratory or superseded, and kept for provenance.

`data/annotations.json` and `data/annotations_v2.json`, read by the superseded `build_sheet*` scripts, come from two runs of the `wf_annotate*.js` workflows, in which an LLM judged whether each claim in the coding frame was resolvable. Both failed validation, and the study uses neither. The two runs agreed on fewer than half of the claims (Cohen's kappa 0.18) and their judgements followed the wording of the prompt. An audit also found that most "resolvable" judgements in the second run overstated what could be checked. They are kept, untracked, in `data/archive_ai_runs/` as the evidence for that finding.

### Archived inputs

Four inputs cannot be recreated by any script. They are kept as data and, because each reveals bands or answers, sealed with the answer key (`data/answer_key/CHECKSUMS.txt`):

- `strip_results.json` and `strip_results_new.json`, the output of the two stripping runs (16 and 28 July 2026). Both ran in the Claude Code workflow runner, with every agent on the model alias `sonnet` (Claude Sonnet), as set in the scripts. The alias does not pin a model version, so a rerun can give different output.
- `scenario_decisions.json`, the decisions on scenario references, made by hand.
- `controls_key.json`, the quality-control items and their answers.

The first stripping run also included the first version of the quality-control items, which were replaced before coding (`ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` §9). Its archived results still contain them.

## The survey

`build_survey_master.py` writes `data/SPI-M_Yardstick_Review_FINAL.json`, which imports into JISC Online Surveys (onlinesurveys.jisc.ac.uk). The survey has 310 pages: an introduction, then one page per item with the statement as a blockquote, its exact date, an assessment (correct, incorrect or cannot tell) and a confidence rating (high, medium or low). Items are titled "Item N", and no probability term or band appears anywhere.

Reviewers work independently and do not see each other's responses until all have finished. For the analysis, export the responses from JISC and join them to `data/survey_item_map.csv` on the question, which gives each item its band and resolvability class.

## Audit trail

`build_lineage.py` and `build_ledger.py` record what happened to every candidate statement between extraction and the survey:

- `data/lineage_candidates.csv`: whether each candidate was included or excluded, and why.
- `data/audit_ledger.csv`: the transformations and final status of every proposition.

## Sharing and embargo

The reviewers are also the co-authors and know the study design, which `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` records and which can be shared. Until they have finished coding, they must not see the answer key: the probability band of each item and the identity of the quality-control items.

The bands appear throughout the working files (`survey_item_map.csv`, `propositions_master.csv`, `audit_ledger.csv` and others). For that reason the repository tracks only the sealed answer key in `data/answer_key/` and ignores the rest of `data/`. The scripts read bands only from the ignored files. Because earlier versions of the scripts contained claims with their bands, the reviewers' agreement covers the history of the repository as well as the answer key.

`data/answer_key/` holds the SHA-256 checksums of the answer key, committed as proof of pre-registration, and a README recording the agreement of the reviewers not to open the key until coding is done. The plaintext answer key is added after coding, and anyone can then check it against the committed checksums. A checksum in a repository the team controls is not tamper-proof on its own; for that, register the checksums externally, for example on OSF (see `data/answer_key/README.md`).

## Code and data availability

Candidate statements were extracted from the source documents and rewritten into blinded propositions with LLM assistance and adversarial verification against the source. The prompts are in the workflow scripts (`scripts/wf_strip.js`, `wf_strip_new.js` and `wf_annotate*.js`), exactly as run, apart from one worked example in `wf_strip.js` that stays a placeholder until coding is complete (`ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` §9). The pipeline and audit trail are in this repository. Suggested wording for the methods section:

> Candidate statements were extracted and rewritten into blinded propositions with LLM assistance and adversarial verification against the source; code and prompts are available at [DOI].

Replace `[DOI]` with an archived snapshot (for example on Zenodo or OSF) taken at submission, so that the citation stays stable.
